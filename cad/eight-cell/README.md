# Eight-cell casing concept

## Restored plunger entry slots

The shared cell base again has six side-entry slots, extending toward the nearest left/right edge. The stem guide and collar-stop entry paths are 2.5 mm wide; matching lower openings are 6.6 mm wide to admit the plunger feet. Outside stop pillars are widened to retain shoulders beside the stem slit. The cuts also clear intervening guide ribs. Load with the reading plate and catch carrier removed and the module undocked. Sampled insertion checks cover each complete plunger, followed by its operating lift and gravity-stop contact. Open slots do not laterally retain loose plungers; check assembled retention with the lid installed. Slot fit, remaining support stiffness and the full physical assembly sequence need a printed trial. Both viewer variants and the rebuilt 3MF/STL package share this source. This restores side access lost during the integrated-guide redesign; it does not restore the old separate guide plates or their central rectangular openings.

All dimensions are millimeters and provisional. Eight positions form one straight row at 50 mm pitch. Eight independently printed cell bases and reading plates fill all positions; there are no empty compartments. Each base/plate is 49.5 mm wide, leaving 0.5 mm between cells. The casing is 406 × 84 × 28 mm, with a 400 mm interior length and underside actuator openings. The single bought top rail is a 1210 × 7 × 7 mm envelope along the rear upper edge. Sliding support geometry is illustrative; bearings, fasteners, retention and rail stiffness are unresolved.

`casing-source.mjs` and `scripts/build-eight-cell.mjs` produce the browser casing, base and plate meshes and their separate concept 3MFs through `npm run build:cad`. Print the base and plate eight times for eight cells. `reset-source.mjs` supplies a fully printed prototype reset bracket, flap and eight repeated carriers, with their own concept 3MFs. The browser reset parts use those same solid exports. Carrier guides and spring stops are integrated into the base, and casing slots let the tabs extend through the front wall. The browser reuses the exact printed plunger and bought 5 mm steel-ball meshes from `servo-design.json`, translated upward 20 mm for the rail cells. The servo bank and printed docking base now reuse the same `createServoGeometry` assembly renderer and `servo-design.json` meshes as the single-cell page. The two drive arms now also use shared solid CAD from `arms-source.mjs`; motor and tray mounting remain concepts. Mesh closure does not establish reliable mechanics.

Isolated-part rulers show local mesh bounding dimensions (X/Y/Z), rounded to 0.001 mm, independently of camera angle. They measure modeled geometry, not manufacturing accuracy or clearances.

The cardboard enclosure uses the same shared nominal 250 × 225 × 116 mm single-cell geometry in both views. In the eight-cell view it is lowered 18 mm below the moving casing; the long rail overhangs it. The shell, roof aperture, rail anchoring and overhang support are visual concepts, not validated construction details.

## Printed gravity-return reset

One fixed bracket clips around the shared docking base's 4 mm front edge (Y=-45, bottom Z=18.5 in the rail view). Its narrow closed fork supports the user-supplied nominal 3 mm × 16 mm nail. The flap has a closed 3.4 mm hub bore; the near cheek has a 3.3 mm entry bore and the far cheek has a provisional 2.9 mm blind retaining socket. A thick, rigid hanging flap rotates about the horizontal Y axis; its center of mass sits below the pivot. There is no flexible station spring, snap bearing or open anchor ring. Forward contact lifts the flap approximately 80 degrees; after the tab passes, gravity is intended to lower it against the broad reverse stop. On return the stop supports the hanging web and the cam moves each catch carrier 6 mm in +Y. Its exit ramp permits the carrier to return without blocking tray travel.

Station pivot: (-25, -60, 87). The follower tip extends to cell Y=-52. The cam crest is at world Y=-46, outside the casing front wall at -42. This allows the flap to swing upward outside the casing. The bracket is supported from the fixed servo bank, not the moving tray. Clearance and contact timing remain provisional.

Print one bracket, one flap and eight carriers, plus the guided cell bases and slotted casing. Place the flap between the fork cheeks and insert the nail from the thin cheek into the far blind socket. The 6 mm-wide hub has 0.75 mm side clearance on each side. The near cheek is 2.5 mm thick, the gap 7.5 mm and the far cheek 7 mm thick, including the point enclosure. The head bears against the near cheek; the shaft is held stationary in the blind socket while the flap turns on it. The nominal 16 mm length is measured under the head including the point; the model assumes a 6 mm head diameter, 1.2 mm head thickness and 2 mm point. These envelope dimensions are provisional. Measure the actual nail, check that its point is enclosed, and test/tune the retaining socket before assembly; do not force a fit that splits the bracket. The nail is labeled bought hardware and excluded from print exports. These are separate parts, not a print-in-place assembly. The carrier retains two 0.8 mm return leaves bearing on base pegs. They and the catches deform illustratively in the viewer. Keep the device upright; gravity return requires a freely moving pivot. Pivot friction, nail retention, settling time, tab/cam contact, spring strain, fatigue and pin drop timing are unvalidated. A one-cell test is required before scaling. Bought servos, guide rail and steel balls remain separate.

Use **Reset detail** and **Slow forward + return demo** in the eight-cell view. The demo stages a raised first cell and replaces the current displayed pattern. The complete cycle still writes all eight cells and resets on return. The animation is prescribed kinematics, not a physics or reliability test.

## Two bearing-linked drive arms

`arms-source.mjs` builds exactly two new printed solids, each exported separately:

- `drive-short-arm_CONCEPT.3mf`: 225 mm motor-to-bearing centers, integral split shaft clamp and offset closed fork. Overall 252 × 46 × 16 mm, including the underside stiffening rib.
- `drive-long-arm_CONCEPT.3mf`: 550 mm bearing-to-tray centers, 20 mm wide, 5.4 mm web plus 3 mm central rib. Overall 570 × 20 × 8.4 mm. It is one piece and needs a large enough printer; do not scale to fit the bed because that changes travel and fits.

These replace the illustrative bars only in the eight-cell page. Existing four-part drive exports are unrelated and unchanged in design. The eight-cell browser and arm 3MFs use the same triangulated solids. `Drive detail`, `Bearing joint` and individual-part isolation expose them; silver bearing/fasteners are separate bought hardware, excluded from the arm 3MFs.

### Reach

The shortened 406 mm casing length is not its travel. Eight cells at 50 mm pitch require 350 mm from first to last writing position, plus 50 mm reset travel: X = −50 through +350 mm. A 225 mm crank has 450 mm theoretical stroke. A 550 mm connecting rod keeps the maximum rod angle at about 24.1 degrees. Motor-to-slider distances run from 350 to 750 mm, leaving 25 mm to both collinear end positions (325 and 775 mm). The fixed motor is now ahead of the tray at X = 745, Y = 0 mm; the tray eye is at local X = 45, Y = 0 mm. Separation falls from 750 to 350 mm on the forward stroke. The short-arm world angle runs from about 156.98 to 34.77 degrees. Reverse over this range; do not command a full revolution against the tray end limits. Homing and physical travel stops are still required outside this two-arm design.

### Bearing and supplied fastener

The user's dimensions establish bearing ID 1/8 inch = 3.175 mm and OD 3/8 inch = 9.525 mm. **Selected width is 5/32 inch = 3.96875 mm**, the recommended [Avid R2ZZ size](https://www.avidrc.com/product/1/bearings/58/18-x-38-x-532-Metal-R2ZZ-bearings.html). This replaces the initial 3.175 mm width assumption after the user asked for a better size than 1/2 inch. The existing purchased bearing has not been measured: confirm its width before printing. Do not use the R2ZZ code alone; check all three dimensions because vendor variants can differ. The long-arm pocket is Ø9.625 mm with a 1 mm shoulder, a Ø6.5 mm lower relief, and a recessed bearing top. Diametral seat clearance is 0.100 mm; printer calibration may require changing it. Retain the outer race in its seat with a small amount of suitable retaining adhesive if needed; keep adhesive off shields and inner race. The fork encloses the joint axially.

The short arm's two 2.5 mm cheeks have a 5.8 mm gap. Integral Ø4.5 mm bosses extend into the arm recesses and contact the inner race. Their contact diameter must be checked against the actual bearing's inner race face; no shield-contact claim can be verified from its ID/OD. The washers bear on the outer faces of the printed cheeks, not on the bearing seals. Stack under the bolt head: washer 0.5 + fork envelope 10.8 + washer 0.5 + nut 2.4 = 14.2 mm. An M3 × 16 under-head length leaves 1.8 mm nominal thread protrusion. Actual washer and nut dimensions, thread engagement and loosening must be checked. M3 is 0.175 mm smaller than the bearing bore, so a threaded bolt is a prototype axle with possible play, not a precision shaft.

Assembly is separate-part assembly, not print-in-place. Seat the bearing in the long arm, then offer the elbow into the open side of the fork. The 32 mm cantilever cheek allows provisional elastic spreading past the bosses (approximately 1.8 mm); this insertion flex is unvalidated. Use a tough material and test the joint region first. Do not force a brittle or resistant fork. Align the boss bores, then install bolt, washers and nut; tighten only enough to seat the inner race while preserving free rotation. Confirm that the long arm turns without rubbing the fork. Final fit may require rebuilding for the measured bearing, not forcing the assembly.

The long arm's other end has a Ø3.3 mm plain pivot hole. It needs an additional tray fastener and a suitable mount/spacer; this is not a second bearing seat. The displayed compact towing eye and raised motor support are concepts, not new print exports.

### PK244M-03BA connection

Oriental Motor's [PK series drawing, C-208 / PDF page 7](https://www.orientalmotor.com/products/pdfs/C_VEXTA/StPk24.pdf) specifies nominal Ø5 mm shaft, 4.5 mm flat, 20 mm front shaft extension, 15 mm flat length, 42 mm frame and 39 mm body for this model. The new short arm has an integrated 12 mm tall split D-bore hub: Ø5.15 mm circular envelope and flat at 2.075 mm from center (nominal flat plus print allowance). Align the flat before installation. This avoids an additional printed shaft adapter. It requires **an additional M3 × 20 clamp bolt, M3 nut and two washers**, separate from the supplied bearing-joint set. The transverse clamp hole is Ø3.3 mm; nut retention is external. Printed clamp grip, creep and shaft fit need testing. The motor itself still needs a rigid frame mount; its face mounting pattern is not part of these arms.

The [manufacturer model page](https://catalog.orientalmotor.com/item/all-categories-legacy-products/tegories-pk-series-2-phase-stepping-motors-legacy-/pk244m-03ba) and catalog list 0.31 N·m bipolar-series / 0.26 N·m unipolar holding torque. At this crank radius the corresponding ideal static force around midstroke is only about 1–1.4 N, before friction and dynamic torque loss. Enough geometric travel does not establish enough force to move the loaded tray. Measure tray drag and reset force before relying on direct drive. Shaft loading, bending/buckling of the long arm, layer strength, clamp slip and fork flex have not been validated.

Run `npm run build:cad`, then `npm run check`. Checks cover connected solids, shared export meshes, closed exported edges, the nominal bolt stack and arm-to-arm/bearing intersection at 81 sampled poses. They do not check physical strength or all assembly collisions. Print the long arm flat with its rib up. The short arm's underside rib and closed fork require an appropriate slicer orientation/support strategy; keep supports out of the race-contact faces and remove them fully before fitting.

The simulation now uses the compact eight-compartment casing centered at X = −175 mm and a matching 396 mm sliding-support envelope. The bought 1210 mm rail, horizontal drive, eight cell locations and −50 to +350 mm movement remain unchanged. The casing sweeps X = −428 through +378 mm (806 mm total envelope). This is a geometric comparison; the animation does not estimate tray friction, moving mass or required motor force.

## Removable front panel

The long front side is a separate printed panel, allowing cells to be inserted in +Y with their projecting reset tabs outside the wall. Three 10 × 3 × 2.2 mm lower tongues locate it. Each end has two 3 × 6.4 × 5 mm panel fingers and one complementary tray finger, forming straight box joints. Mating cuts have 0.15 mm nominal clearance per face; printer accuracy and friction determine the actual fit. No separate locking pins, protruding pin ears or snap flexures are used. The nominal casing envelope returns to 406 × 84 × 28 mm.

Pull the panel toward −Y to disengage the end fingers and lower tongues, load cells from the front, then push the panel back in. The browser Open tray side button separates it by 45 mm and disables tray motion while open. Print the updated casing and panel separately. Solid checks cover closed-panel overlap, finger alignment, panel extraction and all eight cell-base/plate/carrier loading paths. Friction retention during tray acceleration, wear and printed fit are unvalidated; test an end joint before relying on the full panel. Do not glue the removable joint.

A 55 mm motor pedestal is shown as a mounting envelope. It is not an engineered or printable mount. The motor remains ahead of the tray at X=745, Y=0; the horizontal crank layout, 225/550 mm arm centers and −50…350 mm tray travel are preserved. The arm pivots about a vertical bolt; an open hook would not reliably transmit the return push. This arrangement removes the long cantilever from the tray but requires an elevated motor mounting structure. Eye strength, print tolerances, fastening, friction and motor force are unvalidated.

The casing has continuous underside clearance to Z=61 across Y=−34…34, including under its end walls. This clears stationary servo tops and docking pegs between cell positions; the former discrete floor apertures did not. Side ledges remain to support the cells. `check-eight-cell-station.mjs` checks the casing and connector against the fixed base, servo bodies, three horn positions, bracket, axle and prescribed forward/return flap poses at 81 tray positions. ## M3 cell lid fastening

Each cell uses four bought M3 × 8 mm machine screws, M3 × 0.5 thread, without washers in the nominal length stack (32 screws for eight cells). The 2 mm reading plate has Ø3.3 mm clearance holes. Its integral support posts have Ø2.5 mm blind tap pilots, 9 mm deep from the post top. Cut M3 × 0.5 threads after printing; these exports do not contain printed threads, insert pockets, or self-tapping screw provisions. Prepare at least 6 mm of usable thread for the nominal 6 mm screw engagement; use an appropriate blind-hole cutting tap and verify actual depth and screw fit. The pilot diameter follows the conventional M3 cutting-tap range: https://guhring.com/ProductsServices/SizeDetails?EDP=9041550030000 .

Printed fit, thin post walls and thread strength remain provisional, especially in resin. Test one fully cured post before assembling all cells; do not force an untapped hole. Separate lid standoffs are unnecessary because the supports are integral. This update applies to the cell lid connections in both standalone and rail variants; servo hardware and the separate nail reset pivot retain their existing specifications. Older single-cell ZIP packages are not updated and must not be used for this revision; use the freshly rebuilt individual cell-base and reading-plate 3MFs.

## Screw-fastened plunger keepers

Two removable comb strips close the six side-entry slots near their final shaft positions. Print one left and one right keeper per cell (16 strips total for eight cells), plus the revised cell base with four dedicated pillars at X +/-22, Y +/-10 mm. Do not use an earlier base without these pillars. Each strip is fastened with two additional bought M3 x 8 screws: 3.3 mm clearance holes in the keeper and 2.5 mm blind tap pilots, 8 mm deep, through the upper guide into the pillars. Cut M3 x 0.5 threads; allow at least 6 mm usable thread and verify depth before fastening. This adds four screws per cell, 32 for eight cells, beyond the lid screws.

Insert plungers with the lid, carrier and keepers removed and the module undocked. Slide the keepers inward along X, then secure their screws before finishing assembly. For service remove screws and slide the keepers outward with the cell out of its tray. The tongues have 0.2 mm side clearance in the slots; their inner tips leave 0.45 mm nominal shaft clearance. They block direct sideways escape near the shaft rather than only at the outer plate edge. Some lateral play remains; this is not a zero-play bearing. Upper-guide clearance, 2.4 mm operating lift and stop/catch heights are unchanged. Tests check side loading without keepers, keeper removal, 0–3 mm vertical guide travel, and outward shaft interference with installed keepers. Screw/thread strength, flexure, wear, printed fit and all possible escape paths under load still require a physical trial.
